Most of the sky looks the same from one century to the next. A few events have changed it, and people wrote them down. Skyfold can set its clock to those dates and show them where they happened.

## New stars

On 4 July 1054, by the Julian calendar then in use, astronomers of the Song dynasty recorded a "guest star" in Taurus. It was as bright as Venus and could be seen in daylight for 23 days. It stayed visible at night for 642 days.[^tominaga2013] Today the Crab Nebula fills that spot, and at its centre a neutron star spins 30 times a second.

That was a supernova: an exploding star. Five others were seen from Earth with known dates and places. They are SN 1006, the brightest star in recorded history at about magnitude −7.5;[^winkler2003] SN 1181 in Cassiopeia;[^schaefer2023] Tycho Brahe's star of 1572, which showed that the heavens were not unchanging;[^ruizlapuente2004] Kepler's star of 1604, the last one seen in our Galaxy;[^ruizlapuente2017] and Supernova 1987A in the Large Magellanic Cloud. Each brightens and fades as its records say.

## Two neutron stars collide

On 17 August 2017, LIGO and Virgo picked up the gravitational waves of two neutron stars spiralling together, for about 100 seconds. Eleven hours later, telescopes found the collision's light in the galaxy NGC 4993. This was a kilonova: blue at first, then red within days, as newly made heavy elements dimmed its blue light.[^abbott2017][^drout2017]

## Jets near the speed of light

The black hole at the centre of M87 drives a jet thousands of light-years long. Blobs in it seem to cross the sky at six times the speed of light. That is an illusion: they are moving almost straight at us at nearly the speed of light.[^biretta1999] The same speed makes the jet coming towards us hundreds of times brighter than the one going away, which we can barely see.

## Lights over the poles

Particles from the Sun, steered by Earth's magnetic field, make oxygen high in the air glow. The glow is green about 110 km up and red above that. It shines in two ovals around the magnetic poles, which drift slowly over the years.[^whiter2023]
