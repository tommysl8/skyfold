Most planets carry a magnet inside. Molten iron moving in Earth's core, and metallic hydrogen deep in Jupiter and Saturn, keep electric currents flowing, and the currents make magnetic fields that reach far out into space. View › Magnetic field lines draws them, each from a model fitted to what spacecraft and observatories measured.

## Earth and Jupiter

Earth's field is measured all the time by observatories on the ground and by satellites. Every five years the International Association of Geomagnetism and Aeronomy publishes a model of it, the International Geomagnetic Reference Field; its fourteenth version reaches back to 1900.[^igrf] Skyfold draws it for the date. The field's lines leave the southern hemisphere and come back in the northern, so the pole near the Arctic is, in a magnet's terms, a south pole. It drifts: it has moved from Canada towards Siberia over the last century.

Jupiter's field is twenty thousand times stronger in total. Juno has flown over its poles since 2016 and mapped it in detail; the model Skyfold uses, JRM33, comes from its first 33 orbits.[^jrm33] Jupiter's field points the other way from Earth's, and its northern hemisphere has strong, twisted patches that Earth's does not.

## Tipped fields

Voyager 2 found the strangest fields at Uranus in 1986 and Neptune in 1989. Each is tipped far from the planet's spin axis, 60° and 47°, and its centre sits a third to a half of the planet's radius off the planet's own.[^herbert2009][^connerney1991] As the planet turns, its field wobbles round it. Nobody knows exactly how either is turned today: the rotation periods Voyager measured were too uncertain to count the turns across almost forty years.

## The Sun's field

The Sun's field is made near its surface and changes from week to week. Skyfold draws it from the maps of the Sun's surface field that the Solar Dynamics Observatory makes every 27 days, one for each turn of the Sun, by a classic simplification: no currents flow in the corona out to two and a half times the Sun's radius, and beyond that the solar wind drags the field straight out.[^hoeksema] Some lines loop back to the surface over sunspots; others are carried away by the wind. Because the Sun turns while the wind blows outward, those open lines wind into spirals, as water from a turning garden sprinkler does.[^parker1958] Where the field changes direction, a thin, wavy sheet of electric current divides the inward field from the outward.

## What the lines leave out

Each planet's lines are the field of the planet's interior alone. The solar wind presses that field into a cavity, the magnetosphere, and stretches its night side into a tail millions of kilometres long; currents flow on the cavity's edge and through the tail. Skyfold cuts the lines where published models put the edge for an average wind and does not draw the tail or the currents.
