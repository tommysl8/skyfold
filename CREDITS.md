  type from van der Kruit & Freeman (2011), Simien & de Vaucouleurs (1986), Kennicutt (1981), Hernquist (1990),
  Fukugita, Shimasaku & Ichikawa (1995) and Xilouris et al. (1999); dynamical masses of dwarfs after Wolf et al.
  (2010); L* in Ks from Kochanek et al. (2001); the young galaxies' sizes and ultraviolet magnitudes from Tacchella
  et al. (2023), Bunker et al. (2023), Carniani et al. (2024) and Naidu et al. (2026). The facts on the galaxies
  cite their papers.
- Galactic Centre: Sgr A* mass and distance and the orbits of S2, S29, S38 and S55 from GRAVITY Collaboration 2022
  (A&A 657, L12, CC BY 4.0), with general relativity's precession of the pericentre (and, near the black hole, the
  gravitational and orbital shifts of their light in the colours drawn); the K-band extinction towards the
  central parsec (A_Ks = 2.42) from Fritz et al. 2011 (ApJ 737, 73), used for the S-stars' estimated brightness. Other
  published orbits (Gillessen et al. 2017, ApJ 837, 30) are AAS copyright and not used.
- Nebula distances: Hunt & Reffert 2024, Bailer-Jones et al. 2021 (Gaia EDR3) and the papers listed in `nebulae.json`.
- Positions of nebulae: SIMBAD (CDS, Strasbourg), Wenger et al. 2000, A&AS 143, 9.
- Spiral arms of the location trail: the fits of Reid et al. 2019 (ApJ 885, 131) and their tangency priors (Bronfman
  et al. 2000), in `src/sim/galaxy/arms.ts`.
- Black holes, the physics (`src/physics/schwarzschild.ts`, `schwarzschildTables.ts`, `lensPoint.ts`, `geodesics.ts`
  and their shaders): the Schwarzschild solution in the forms of Darwin (1959, Proc. R. Soc. A 249, 180) and Carlson's
  symmetric elliptic integrals (Carlson 1995, Numer. Algorithms 10, 13; NIST DLMF chapter 19); the shadow of Synge
  (1966, MNRAS 131, 463); the exact lens equation of Perlick (2004, Living Rev. Relativ. 7, 9; Phys. Rev. D 69,
  064017); the finite-source factor of Gould (1994, ApJ 421, L71); the raindrop and the free-fallers' time of Hamilton
  & Lisle (2008, Am. J. Phys. 76, 519); the spin note from the shadow of a spinning black hole (Bardeen 1973, in
  *Black Holes*, Les Houches), computed for Sgr A*'s viewing angle. Checked against the project's own reference in
  `scripts/schwarzschild/`, which shares no code with them.
- Black holes, Cygnus X-1's accretion disc (`src/physics/thinDisk.ts`, `src/render/disk/`): the thin disc of Novikov &
  Thorne (1973, in *Black Holes*, Les Houches) with the flux of Page & Thorne (1974, ApJ 191, 499) in the closed form
  for no spin of Luminet (1979, A&A 75, 228), whose pictures of such a disc are its reference; the disc's luminosity, 2 %
  of Eddington, from Zhao et al. (2021, ApJ 908, 117, Table 2); its outer edge, a model choice, after Palit, Janiuk &
  Czerny (2020, ApJ 904, 21) and the tidal limit of Paczyński (1977, ApJ 216, 822). Checked against the project's own
  reference in `scripts/thin-disk/`.
- Black holes, the pictures: the Event Horizon Telescope's images of Sgr A* and M87* (2017 data), published by ESO as
  [eso2208-eht-mwa](https://www.eso.org/public/images/eso2208-eht-mwa/) and
  [eso1907a](https://www.eso.org/public/images/eso1907a/), credit "EHT Collaboration", [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/):
  both black holes' cards credit them and link to them. The ring sizes quoted with them are from EHT Collaboration
  2019 (ApJL 875, L1) and 2022 (ApJL 930, L12).
- Black holes, positions and magnitudes of the companions and the lone lens: SIMBAD (CDS), retrieved 28 September
  2026.
- Deep-sky catalogues: OpenNGC by Mattia Verga (CC BY-SA 4.0); the ATNF Pulsar Catalogue, "Manchester, R. N., Hobbs,
  G. B., Teoh, A. & Hobbs, M., Astron. J., 129, 1993-2006 (2005)", https://www.atnf.csiro.au/research/pulsar/psrcat;
  Ranasinghe & Leahy 2022 (ApJ 940, 63) and Green 2025 (J. Astrophys. Astron. 46, 14) for the supernova remnants;
  "This research has made use of data or software obtained from the Gravitational Wave Open Science Center (gwosc.org),
  a service of the LIGO Scientific Collaboration, the Virgo Collaboration, and KAGRA."
- The Milky Way's magnetic field: the UF23 "base" model of Unger & Farrar 2024 (ApJ 970, 95); the field over the
  sky from WMAP's nine-year K-band polarisation (NASA / WMAP Science Team). Planck's 353 GHz polarisation (Planck
  Legacy Archive, "ESA, Planck Collaboration") can be drawn instead by `scripts/build-field-sky.mjs --source planck`, but
  ESA's archive terms (CC BY-NC 3.0 IGO) allow only non-commercial reuse, and this project ships only data free for
  any use: that texture is not shipped.
